"Hello, I'm Mohammad Firas Sada"

2026-09-20

To be clear about the name: I go by Mohammad. Some people in my life call me Firas, both are me, and I answer to both, but the site will use Mohammad.

Now that that's settled: I'm a research networking systems engineer at ESnet, the Energy Sciences Network, at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory. I joined in September 2026, working remotely, the same way I worked at the San Diego Supercomputer Center before that.

Remote is the whole story: the problems changed, the desk didn't. My job now is data movement for high-energy physics: software-defined networks for science, SENSE orchestration, and the plumbing between the transfer tools researchers actually use.

Before ESnet I led the SEAM project at SDSC, worked on the National Research Platform's Kubernetes substrate, taught its intro courses, and did years of evenings on P4 and FPGAs. Some of that turned into papers.

Some of it just turned into repos nobody reads. Things I care about: shared infrastructure should be usable by the person who needs it, not just the person who built it.

Networks should be programmable rather than merely configurable. And if you're going to compare models or networks or clusters, measure honestly and show the method, because the tools exist now and the excuses don't.

This blog is mostly about data movement and programmable networks, with the occasional paper walkthrough. I'll also post when I break something and learn from it, since that tends to be the useful content.

Code on [GitHub](https://github.com/groundsada), papers on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13185), updates on [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/msada).
