"Teaching the National Research Platform"

2025-09-02

The National Research Platform is a stretched, multi-tenant Kubernetes environment where researchers at US campuses can run science jobs without their own cluster. Building it is interesting.

Teaching it turned out to be more interesting, and I say that honestly. In 2025 I became an instructor for SDSC's on-demand learning series.

Two courses: Introduction to the National Research Platform, and Intermediate Kubernetes. The first one is the pitch: what the platform is, who it's for, how to get your first job running.

The second one is the unglamorous truth: multi-tenancy, resource management, and the sharp edges that show up when a hundred researchers share one thing. I teach because I don't understand a system until I've explained it to someone who doesn't care about my system.

Every course cycle I found at least one bug in my own mental model, usually because a student asked a question that only made sense if you assumed things should work. That's a useful failure mode.

And there's the other reason. Shared infrastructure only works if the people it serves can actually use it.

The students I remember most are researchers from fields with nothing to do with CS, walking in with a dataset and a deadline, walking out with a running job. When I was in college I was grinding USACO problem sets, and the idea that I'd end up teaching distributed systems was not on any plan I had.

The NRP is a national asset. If you're at a US research institution and you haven't tried it, the intro course is free on SDSC's learning site.

You should.
