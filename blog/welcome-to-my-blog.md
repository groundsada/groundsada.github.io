# Hey, I'm Firas — welcome

Let me skip the résumé for a second: I'm the guy who thinks the most interesting place in the network is wherever a scientific dataset is trying to get to another lab before 5 p.m.

I'm a research networking systems engineer at **ESnet** (Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory) — I work on moving high-energy physics data across the country, on software-defined networks that get scheduled like the transfers they carry. Before that I was at the **San Diego Supercomputer Center**, where I led the SEAM project, helped build the National Research Platform's Kubernetes substrate, taught its intro courses, and spent many late nights making P4 programs run on FPGAs.

## Three things I care about

1. **Making science infrastructure work for the people using it.** The best k8s cluster is the one a biologist can use without asking for help. This is why I teach, and why I obsess over observability.
2. **Networks that can be programmed, not just configured.** I've written P4, compiled it to FPGAs, and I still get a little giddy when packet processing leaves the switch ASIC.
3. **Small, honest measurements.** My PEARC '26 paper is basically a love letter to (checks notes) Naive Bayes — because on a good day, it beats a trillion-parameter model at a fraction of the cost, and you can actually prove it.

## What you'll find here

- The occasional deep-ish dive — data movement, SENSE, programmable data planes, model evaluation
- Stories from the road: what it's like to build shared infrastructure for research computing
- The side projects: a WAR calculator in R, image-generation experiments, competitive-programming warmups
- Honest notes when I break something and learn from it

You can find my code on [GitHub](https://github.com/groundsada), papers on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13185), and updates on [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/msada). Thanks for reading.
