# Hello, I'm Firas

I'm a research networking systems engineer at ESnet, the Energy Sciences Network, at Lawrence Berkeley National Laboratory. Since September 2026 I've been working on moving high-energy physics data around the country: software-defined networks for science, SENSE orchestration, and the plumbing between the transfer tools researchers actually use.

Before ESnet I was at the San Diego Supercomputer Center (UC San Diego). There I led the SEAM project, worked on the National Research Platform's Kubernetes substrate, taught its intro courses, and spent years of evenings on P4 and FPGAs. Some of that turned into papers. Some of it just turned into repos nobody reads.

I care about a few things in particular. Shared infrastructure should be usable by the person who needs it, not just the person who built it. Networks should be programmable rather than merely configurable. And if you're going to compare models or networks or clusters, you should measure honestly and show the method, because the tools exist now and the excuses don't.

This blog is mostly about data movement and programmable networks, with the occasional paper walkthrough. I'll also post when I break something and learn from it, since that tends to be the useful content.

You can find my code on [GitHub](https://github.com/groundsada), my papers on [arXiv](https://arxiv.org/abs/2609.13185), and my professional updates on [LinkedIn](https://www.linkedin.com/in/msada).
